# Rendering (src/render, excluding ui/)

**Purpose:** retro first-person view of sim state, drawn only on the GPU (`gpu/`, docs/GPU-RENDER.md). Read-only on the sim.
**Never:** polygon models or external art; hand-drawn pictures or fixed colour lists in render code; write sim state;
allocate in hot loops. Look is **sprites + procedural textures**; perspective on held items is a sprite transform.

- `adaptiveRes.ts` lowers/raises the line count to hold the wished fps (setting 'Keep it smooth'; numbers in `ADAPTIVE`).
- `viewport.ts` low-res frame size scaled cleanly. `lighting.ts` gathers the lights (and a 1 m grid for the held item).
- `raycaster/` (CPU helpers the GPU reads): far cached LOD blocks (`terrainLod.ts`, `lodLooks.ts`, ~7 km), `tileBillboards.ts`,
  `cloudShadow.ts`, `camera.ts`. Clouds and ground mist follow the world's moisture (`airMoisture` in `content/skyField.ts`, set by `Simulation`; layer `wet`).
- The air is ONE grid of cells (`airCells.ts`, sizes in `SkyDef.air`): every sky layer (cloud, mist) writes into it through `airColumn` (content/skyField.ts), the same rule rain, cloud shade and in-cloud fog read. Levels double in width every `air.lod` cells of distance (height stops at the layers' band / `tall`, so far cells are wide flat clumps); cells are stored wrapped, so moving fills only the new ones; far levels refresh less often. The GPU walks the cells nearest first (one pass, right order) and treats each clump as one solid shape: the face the ray enters by sets its look for the whole clump (no inside faces or seams). A new cloud/mist kind is a new layer in `content/sky.ts`, nothing else.
- A rebuilt level dithers in over `air.blend` s (two texture slots per level); whole rebuilds only when a layer cover or the rain moved by `air.change`. A clump's entry face comes from its cell box, not the previous step.
- All air moves with the wind (one drifting frame, no per-layer speed). Finest air cells are 1 m (the 3D layer's cell size); levels cross-fade by an ordered dither. Smoke is the live level of the air (`airSmoke.ts`, `SkyDef.smoke`): the matter state look `smoke` (fire) feeds the one 1 m cell above each fire; it rises slowly, spreads a little sideways and thins in steps, only where smoke is, so one fire makes a thin column and many fires a cloud.
- Fog (`env.ts`): the fog colour is the sky's horizon colour, or the cloud you stand in (shorter sight, cloud colour); clouds use `Env.cloudLit` (same light as the overcast sky) and haze over `fogDist × sky.haze`.
- LOD blocks of every level (incl. the 1 m one the GPU textured levels fade into) get changed tiles painted over them (`terrainLodPaint.paintChunk`); the GPU refills its fine levels when `lod.painted` moves.
- `materials/` paints surfaces into textures.
- Shadows: the solid field (`gpuSolid.ts`) keeps each 3D cell's shape byte, and shadow rays test shaped cells against their real pieces (`SHAPE_SHADER`, shared with the structure pass), so roofs and slopes shade by their true shape. Sprite shadow cards mirror when the eye is on the light's side, so the shadow matches the drawn picture from both sides.
- Neighbour ground tiles and 3D cell faces (any kinds, same kind too) blend `SURFACE_MIX.blend` texels (5; 3D cells `cellBlend` 2) to either side of their edge by one shared GLSL rule `edgeLean` (gpuSurfaces.ts), each cell's colour drift fading into the next; centres stay pure. `surfPatches` adds soft world-space light/dark and warm/cool patches (`SURFACE_MIX.patch*`) on both.
- Surfaces are TEX = 16 texels a side, 16 to the metre on ground, tile walls and 3D cells. A surface (`content/surfaces.ts`) is a base colour plus layers, the reusable components; a layer that covers only some texels (speckle, lines, mortar, seams, patches) is its own region (atlas alpha), and the shader's `surf(..., key)` drifts each region's hue/richness/lightness per placed cell by the surface's `drift` (default `SURFACE_DRIFT`; packed in a row under the atlas). Randoms come from an integer hash of the cell (`surfRand`), never `sin`, so nothing bands far from the origin. A surface with `spin` turns/mirrors per cell; a layer's `turn` paints it crosswise; wave frequencies snap to whole waves per tile so every surface tiles. Far away the atlas mips (`SURFACE_MIX.lods`, picked per pixel by `surfLodFrom`) replace texels that would shimmer; near each switch the next mip fades in (`surfLodT`), so levels never meet in a line. Noise octaves and `noise`/`patches` cells stay 4 texels or finer (a tile-sized blob repeats per tile and reads as a grid or stripes; `validateContent` enforces it). The atlas height is padded to whole slots so every mip halves exactly (an odd height bled slots into each other on Windows GPUs: a line along every tile edge far off).
- Matter states (soot, wet, oil, frost, fire) are never drawn by swapping materials: `content/matterStates.ts` says how each blends (shade, cover colour or surface, glint, glow, rain), `gpu/gpuStates.ts` `blendStates` draws them on ground, tile walls and 3D cells; far land gets `stateLooks.tintFar`.
- `entities/`: ONE sprite assembler for every picture built from parts (creatures, people, trees, rocks, plants, props, items): `artGrow.ts` builds an individual of an `ArtDef` (fixed parts = joints, grown parts = growth rules), `assembler.ts` poses it (things with a `gait` walk/rest/strike/lie down); motion is data on the recipe: joint `stride` (swing, phase, `lift` so a step shows head on), `idle`, `strike`, gait `bounce`/`hop`/`sway`, `attack` lean/lunge; held items and worn shells hang from limbs, so they move with them and paints it via `bodyPaint.ts` in an `ArtBank`; `artSprites.ts` picks individual, pose and side for entities, `tileBillboards.ts` for tiles. items (`itemArt.ts`: one 20 px picture per item from its art pieces `item.<part>`, dressed by material/quality/wear/reach via `DressDef` colours, features and `stretch`; never mirrored, edges on the left; look worked out in `itemLook.ts`), billboards (`spriteRenderer.ts`). Colour regions are filled from
  palettes at draw time (`palette.ts`). Missing sprite = magenta checks, never a crash.
- Gear on bodies (`gearArt.ts`): parts marked `wear` (a body region or 'hand') show what the entity wears and holds. A worn piece is a shell over each marked part, fit by the region's `fit` (content/gear.ts), coloured and textured by its material (`GearMaterialDef.surface`; dress `surfaces`), so leather and iron share one silhouette. The held item is its picture's pieces at the 'hand' part, sized by `GEAR.hand.hold`. Item pictures get a dark ring outside so they read on any background.
- `vfx/particles.ts` + `particleKinds.ts`: spell/effect looks come from rune `look` data.
- Held item: `entities/viewModel.ts` warps the item's picture to a pose; `viewPose.ts` moves it: each swing is a path in
  `content/viewmodel.ts` `arcs` (hand curves about a pivot, blade turns from drawn back through the hit to the follow-through,
  tip lags then whips; left/right sweeps keep hand and blade level so they read apart from the chop), and wind-up/rest poses settle smoothly (`motion`), so changing the aim while charging glides. `viewFx.ts` = flame motes.
- Blow effects (sparks, dust, blood, embers) are world motes from `vfx/particles.ts`, chosen by material and weapon data; the held item only kicks.


## Add / change
- New creature/plant/prop look: one `ArtDef` entry (`content/looks.ts`, `forms.ts`, `smallForms.ts`, `props.ts` all feed `CONTENT.art`; colours in `palettes.ts`), no render code.
- New draw pass: read state + content only; keep per-frame work budgeted.

## Checks
`npm run check:boot` boots the bundle in a browser (not in CI; run it before reporting renderer or startup changes).
A new `Renderer` is made on every world swap (join, load): always `dispose()` the old one first.
`scripts/artSmoke.ts`, `skySmoke.ts`; `npx tsc --noEmit`.

Sprite views (8-way creatures, props) and left/right facing are picked from where the camera STANDS relative to the thing (`sideFrom` in raycaster/camera.ts), never from where it is turned. When the frame's painting budget is spent, a stand-in picture is always one from the SAME side (another moment of the pose, or another pose); a side never drawn is painted anyway (a stand-in from another side made creatures face the wrong way). Each billboard quad also turns to face the camera's position (gpuSprites VS), so turning or tilting the view never swings it round.
Each sprite picture sits in the GPU atlas ONCE, its colour-region pixels stored as (region, shade) codes; each individual's colours are a row in a palette texture (`GPU_SPRITES`). The atlas and palette only start over between frames, never while a frame's billboards are queued (that once made dense forests shuffle pictures). Flora is collected last, so the billboard cap drops trees before creatures.
