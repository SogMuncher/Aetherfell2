# Rendering: sprites, gear on bodies, held item (part of docs/RENDER.md)

- `entities/`: ONE sprite assembler for every picture built from parts (creatures, people, trees, rocks, plants, props, items): `artGrow.ts` builds an individual of an `ArtDef` (fixed parts = joints, grown parts = growth rules), `assembler.ts` poses it (things with a `gait` walk/rest/strike/lie down); motion is data on the recipe: joint `stride` (swing, phase, `lift` so a step shows head on, `fold` for knees and elbows), `idle`, gait `bounce`/`hop`/`sway`, `attack` lean/lunge, `build` (lanky/stocky per shape); body plans with two-part limbs live in `content/bodyPlans.ts`. Swings (each direction) and the throw are `content/moves.ts`: per joint role (`act`: arm, fore, off, torso, head, neck) a wind-up, release and follow-through turn; `artSprites.moveFrame` picks the picture from the sim's charge/swing timing (release on `strikeAt`), the throw from the 'thrown' event; held items and worn shells hang from limbs, so they move with them and paints it via `bodyPaint.ts` in an `ArtBank`; `artSprites.ts` picks individual, pose and side for entities, `tileBillboards.ts` for tiles. items (`itemArt.ts`: one 20 px picture per item from its art pieces `item.<part>`, dressed by material/quality/wear/reach via `DressDef` colours, features and `stretch`; never mirrored, edges on the left; look worked out in `itemLook.ts`), billboards (`spriteRenderer.ts`). Colour regions are filled from
  palettes at draw time (`palette.ts`). Missing sprite = magenta checks, never a crash.
- Gear on bodies (`gearArt.ts`): parts marked `wear` (a body region or 'hand') show what the entity wears and holds. A worn piece is a shell over each marked part, fit by the region's `fit` (content/gear.ts), coloured and textured by its material (`GearMaterialDef.surface`; dress `surfaces`), so leather and iron share one silhouette. The held item is its bag picture on a flat card (`ArtPart.card`) gripped at the 'hand' part (`GEAR.hand.hold`), stamped facing the viewer and turned to point along the arm (`paintFinish.ts`, grip found by `itemCard.ts`); worn armour stays 8-sided. Item pictures get a dark ring outside so they read on any background.
- Held item: `entities/viewModel.ts` warps the item's picture to a pose; `viewPose.ts` moves it: each swing is a path in
  `content/viewmodel.ts` `arcs` (hand curves about a pivot, blade turns from drawn back through the hit to the follow-through,
  tip lags then whips; left/right sweeps keep hand and blade level so they read apart from the chop), and wind-up/rest poses settle smoothly (`motion`), so changing the aim while charging glides. `viewFx.ts` = flame motes.
- Open glider in your own view: `entities/viewGlider.ts` paints a cloth canopy overhead (snaps open, ripples with speed, banks into turns, hem glows in its trail colour); `vfx/glideTrails.ts` lays the light trail from each wingtip (`GlideDef.trail`).
- Blow effects (sparks, dust, blood, embers) are world motes from `vfx/particles.ts`, chosen by material and weapon data; the held item only kicks.

Sprite views (8-way creatures, props) and left/right facing are picked from where the camera STANDS relative to the thing (`sideFrom` in raycaster/camera.ts), never from where it is turned. When the frame's painting budget is spent, a stand-in picture is always one from the SAME side (another moment of the pose, or another pose); a side never drawn is painted anyway (a stand-in from another side made creatures face the wrong way). The budget opens once per frame at the start of `spriteRenderer.collect`, before creatures pick (opened later, creatures always found it spent and froze at one picture per side; `artSmoke` checks this in the real draw order). Each billboard quad also turns to face the camera's position (gpuSprites VS), so turning or tilting the view never swings it round.
Each sprite picture sits in the GPU atlas ONCE, its colour-region pixels stored as (region, shade) codes; each individual's colours are a row in a palette texture (`GPU_SPRITES`). The atlas and palette only start over between frames, never while a frame's billboards are queued (that once made dense forests shuffle pictures). Flora is collected last, so the billboard cap drops trees before creatures.
