# Rendering: sky, air, clouds, fog (part of docs/RENDER.md)

- The air is ONE grid of cells (`airCells.ts`, sizes in `SkyDef.air`): every sky layer (cloud, mist) writes into it through `airColumn` (content/skyField.ts), the same rule rain, cloud shade and in-cloud fog read. Levels double in width every `air.lod` cells of distance (height stops at the layers' band / `tall`, so far cells are wide flat clumps); cells are stored wrapped, so moving fills only the new ones; far levels refresh less often. The GPU walks the cells nearest first (one pass, right order) and treats each clump as one solid shape: the face the ray enters by sets its look for the whole clump (no inside faces or seams). A new cloud/mist kind is a new layer in `content/sky.ts`, nothing else.
- Every new build of a level (moved or rebuilt) dithers in over `air.blend` s (two texture slots per level), over the coarser level where its old build did not reach; a level the eye left behind gets `air.rush` × the budget and goes ahead of weather refills (which wait); whole rebuilds only when a layer cover or the rain moved by `air.change`. A clump's entry face comes from its cell box, not the previous step.
- All air moves with the wind (one drifting frame, no per-layer speed). Finest air cells are 1 m (the 3D layer's cell size); levels cross-fade by an ordered dither. Smoke is the live level of the air (`airSmoke.ts`, `SkyDef.smoke`): the matter state look `smoke` (fire) feeds the one 1 m cell above each fire; it rises slowly, spreads a little sideways and thins in steps, only where smoke is, so one fire makes a thin column and many fires a cloud.
- Fog (`env.ts`): the fog colour is the sky's horizon colour, or the cloud you stand in (shorter sight, cloud colour); clouds use `Env.cloudLit` (same light as the overcast sky) and haze over `fogDist × sky.haze`.
- Floating air motes (`gpu/gpuAir.ts`) drift with their `AirDef.drift` plus the weather wind under open sky. Speed lines (`gpu/gpuSpeedLines.ts`) start at 7 m/s, full at 15.
